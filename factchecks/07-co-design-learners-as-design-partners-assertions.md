### EVIDENCE — CONFIRMED (with quantitative note)
**Assertion type:** BASIC
**Sentence:** "In interleaving studies, most participants rated the less effective massed schedule the better one *even after* interleaving had just outperformed it on their own test (Kornell & Bjork 2008)."
**Claim checked:** Kornell & Bjork 2008 — participants judged massing better despite spacing/interleaving winning on their own test.
**Site visited:** journals.sagepub.com; web.williams.edu (author PDF); Semantic Scholar.
**Finding:** CONFIRMED. Kornell, N., & Bjork, R. A. (2008). "Learning concepts and categories: Is spacing the 'enemy of induction'?" *Psychological Science*, 19(6), 585–592. Spaced/interleaved study improved inductive category learning, yet ~71% of participants judged massing more effective (only ~12% correctly judged spacing better) — even after their own test showed the opposite. Strong support.
**Expert review needed:** No.
**Suggested reference:** Kornell, N., & Bjork, R. A. (2008). *Psychological Science*, 19(6), 585–592.
**Notes:** Strictly this study contrasts spaced vs. massed (interleaving-adjacent). The chapter's "interleaving studies" gloss is a mild generalization but within accepted usage of this paradigm.
