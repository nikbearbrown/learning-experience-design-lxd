**Book:** learning-experience-design-lxd  
**Created:** 2026-06-07  
**Purpose:** Small, explicit list of checks to run immediately before final manuscript freeze. Inline `[verify]` and `[contested]` tags have been removed from chapter prose; unresolved or time-sensitive items live here.

## Check Before Freeze

1. **Chapter 2 — LXD bibliometric claim**
   - **Current prose:** LXD is described as borrowing from UX, instructional design, educational technology, and the learning sciences without yet consolidating around its own canon.
   - **Question:** Can we identify a specific bibliometric review of the LXD literature that supports the stronger "borrowing without consolidation" claim?
   - **Action:** If no source is found, keep the softened prose and do not restore the stronger bibliometric wording.

2. **Chapter 2 — no direct LXD-vs-ID comparative study**
   - **Current prose:** "I am aware of no direct comparative study..."
   - **Question:** Has any direct comparative study appeared comparing LXD-trained and ID-trained designers on process, artifact quality, or learning outcomes?
   - **Action:** Re-run a focused ERIC / Google Scholar search before freeze. Keep the hedged "I am aware of no..." wording unless a study is found.

3. **Chapter 4 / Chapter 15 — task-value single-predictor ranking**
   - **Current prose:** Task value is central to cognitive engagement; exact single-predictor ranking and variance percentages are not treated as settled.
   - **Question:** Does a primary source support the stronger claim that task value alone is the strongest predictor of elaboration/deep processing, stronger than self-efficacy and self-regulation?
   - **Action:** If not, keep the softened wording and remove any leftover "strongest predictor" phrasing in tables or summaries.

4. **Chapter 6 — spacing / structural reflection citation**
   - **Current prose:** Gaps between sessions are described as structural reflection where spacing research may be doing silent work.
   - **Question:** Which systematic review or meta-analysis should anchor this exact claim?
   - **Action:** Add the chosen spacing-reference citation to Chapter 6 references before freeze.

5. **Chapter 10 — Zeng gamification precision details**
   - **Current prose:** Zeng et al. 2024 is used for 22 studies and Hedges' g = 0.782.
   - **Question:** Do the full paper and forest plot confirm the pooled sample size and exact count of negative studies?
   - **Action:** Keep headline figures unless contradicted; only use "two negative studies" or the exact pooled n if the full text confirms them.

6. **Chapter 10 — leaderboard review anchor**
   - **Current prose:** Review literature suggests leaderboard benefits concentrate among high performers and competitive-orientation learners, with discouragement risks for persistently low-ranked learners.
   - **Question:** What exact systematic review, ideally the *Journal of Computer Assisted Learning* review originally noted, should anchor this?
   - **Action:** Either add the exact citation or soften to "some studies suggest..." before freeze.

7. **Chapter 11 — procedural immersive-training review**
   - **Current prose:** Reviews of immersive procedural training find benefits where real practice is dangerous, expensive, or rare.
   - **Question:** What exact 2024 *Computers & Education* or equivalent review should anchor the procedural-training claim?
   - **Action:** Add exact citation or broaden the wording to "simulation-training literature."

8. **Chapter 12 — current model sycophancy / guardrail bypass behavior**
   - **Current prose:** Current models may cave to persistent requests, and this should be rechecked at manuscript freeze.
   - **Question:** As of freeze date, do current frontier model evaluations still support the claim that sycophancy or persistence-based guardrail bypass remains common enough to shape product guidance?
   - **Action:** Recheck current model-behavior literature and policy guidance; if the behavior has materially improved, soften the passage.

9. **Chapter 13 — clickstream primary-study anchor**
   - **Current prose:** Clickstream features such as persistence, consistency, and time-on-task are statistically predictive of course performance at population scale.
   - **Question:** Which one or two primary MOOC / learning-analytics studies should anchor this synthesis?
   - **Action:** Add the named references before freeze.

10. **Chapter 14 — Alliger et al. exact coefficients**
    - **Current prose:** Affective reactions to training correlate weakly, near zero by some estimates, with learning.
    - **Question:** What exact Alliger et al. 1997 coefficient should be cited, and how should the affective-reaction vs utility-reaction distinction be stated?
    - **Action:** Keep the hedge. Do not flatten the claim into "all reactions correlate near zero with learning."
