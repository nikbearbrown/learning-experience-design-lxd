### [EVIDENCE] — CONFIRMED-with-candidate (resolves a `[verify]` flag)
**Assertion type:** BASIC
**Sentence:** "A 2024 meta-analysis finds positive overall effects exactly under that alignment condition [verify]." / Evidence Box: "Embodied action helps when functionally aligned with task | 2024 meta-analysis [verify]."
**Claim checked:** Whether a 2024 embodied-learning meta-analysis exists and reports a positive overall effect moderated by task alignment/congruence.
**Site visited:** ScienceDirect (Learning and Individual Differences, S1041608024001572); Frontiers in Psychology (10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1658797); Nature Human Behaviour (s41562-025-02152-2).
**Finding:** A real 2024 meta-analysis exists and supports the positive-overall-effect half of the claim: Lyu & Deng (2024), "Effectiveness of embodied learning on learning performance: A meta-analysis based on the cognitive load theory perspective," Learning and Individual Differences 116, 102564 — g ≈ 0.52, 95% CI [0.37, 0.66]. A 2025 Frontiers meta-analysis (66 effect sizes, 2010–2025) reports g = 0.406 [0.264, 0.548]. The *positive overall effect* is well-supported. The *specific* "functionally aligned/congruent action" moderator is robust in the broader embodied-cognition literature but was not the headline moderator coded in Lyu & Deng (which coded discipline, level, duration, sample size). 
**Expert review needed:** Light — confirm which meta-analysis the author intends and whether it codes an alignment/congruence moderator specifically, or soften "exactly under that alignment condition" to attribute the moderator to the wider literature.
**Suggested reference:** Reference 11 (candidate).
**Notes:** The chapter already flags this as provisional ("Citation to be confirmed before manuscript freeze; treat as provisional"). The flag is appropriate and the underlying claim survives; only the moderator attribution needs tightening.
