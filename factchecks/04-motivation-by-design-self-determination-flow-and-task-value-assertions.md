## ⚠️ Critical — Requires Immediate Expert Review

**1. Wang et al. 2024 SDT meta-analysis is OVERSTATED (CONTRADICTED in part).** The chapter says the meta-analysis found that supporting basic needs "reliably improved motivation, engagement, and academic outcomes — with effects that held across school levels and cultures." The actual paper reports a *partially* significant effect (g = 0.58 for intrinsic motivation) and **no significant overall effect on relatedness** (g = 0.44, p > 0.05). The blanket "reliably improved... held across school levels and cultures" overstates a result the authors themselves qualify as partial. This is load-bearing because the chapter leans on it to claim SDT is "one of the better-replicated intervention bodies." Recommend softening to "partially significant, with autonomy and competence effects more robust than relatedness." See Full Findings.

## Full Findings

### [EVIDENCE / STAT] — CONTRADICTED (overstated)
**Assertion type:** EMPHATIC (emphatic + unhedged = COMBINATION, top priority)
**Sentence:** "A meta-analysis of SDT-based interventions in education found that supporting students' basic psychological needs reliably improved motivation, engagement, and academic outcomes — with effects that held across school levels and cultures (Wang et al. 2024)."
**Claim checked:** That a 2024 SDT meta-analysis found reliable improvement across motivation, engagement, and academic outcomes, robust across levels and cultures.
**Site visited:** ScienceDirect (Learning and Instruction, S0023969024000572); selfdeterminationtheory.org PDF (2024_WangWangEtAl_MetaEdu.pdf).
**Finding:** The meta-analysis exists — Wang et al. (2024), a systematic review and meta-analysis of SDT-based interventions in education, 36 intervention studies, N ≈ 11,792. But its results are more qualified than the chapter states: intrinsic motivation g = 0.58 (p < 0.01, "partially significant"), and **relatedness g = 0.44 was NOT significant (p > 0.05)**. Autonomy support was the most consistent. The chapter's "reliably improved motivation, engagement, and academic outcomes — with effects that held across school levels and cultures" is an overgeneralization of a partial result.
**Expert review needed:** Yes. Rewrite to reflect partial significance and the non-significant relatedness effect.
**Suggested reference:** Reference 1.
**Notes:** Ironically, the non-significant relatedness result *strengthens* the chapter's own thesis that relatedness is "the most neglected need... the hardest to fake at scale." The honest version is more on-message than the overstated one.
