## A. Conceptual foundations

### SDT maps motivation quality to autonomy, competence, and relatedness
Self-determination theory maps motivation quality to autonomy, competence, and relatedness support. Durable motivation needs meaningful choice, visible progress, and social belonging, not just pressure or reward.

**Common misconception:** Motivation mainly comes from rewards.

**Worked example:** Use the studio project. Ask what design decision changes if this concept is true. Then create a before/after artifact and a one-sentence Evidence Disclosure. For this chapter, the simplest case is: Leaderboard spike followed by below-baseline participation.

### Task value must be visible in the design
Task value asks whether learners can see utility, identity relevance, interest, and cost. Authentic tasks, visible future use, and explicit transfer moments help learners understand why effort is worth spending.

**Common misconception:** Motivation mainly comes from rewards.

### Flow is useful but fragile as a measurement target
Flow can name challenge-skill balance and absorption, but it is hard to measure cleanly and can be confused with entertainment. Treat it as one possible experience quality, not evidence of learning.
