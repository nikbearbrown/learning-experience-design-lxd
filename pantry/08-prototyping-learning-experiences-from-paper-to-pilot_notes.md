## A. Conceptual foundations

### Prototype fidelity should match the uncertainty being tested
Prototype fidelity is a research decision. Choose paper, storyboard, clickable mockup, wizard-of-oz, or pilot module according to the riskiest unanswered question.

**Common misconception:** Higher fidelity is always better.

**Worked example:** Use the studio project. Ask what design decision changes if this concept is true. Then create a before/after artifact and a one-sentence Evidence Disclosure. For this chapter, the simplest case is: Polished branching simulation fails in first learner test.

### Learning prototypes test misconception, load, feedback, and motivation
Learning prototype tests capture what learners think the rule is, where load spikes, what feedback changes, and whether motivation is supported.

## E. Teaching considerations

**Likely sticking point:** Students will tend to turn this chapter into a checklist. Force them to name the evidence status, the learner data source, and the decision that changes.

**Exercise ideas:**
- Diagnose a flawed artifact from the opening case and label the evidence problem.
- Apply the chapter method to the Track A statistics course.
- Track B: apply the method to the student's own project and write a short Evidence Disclosure.

**LLM guardrail:** Permit AI to generate alternatives or critique clarity, but require the student to provide the evidence label, learner-data source, and final design judgment. Do not let AI replace learner contact, delayed assessment, or ethical review.
