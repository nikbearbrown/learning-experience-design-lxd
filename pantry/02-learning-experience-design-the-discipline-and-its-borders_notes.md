## A. Conceptual foundations

### LXD is an orienting synthesis rather than a fully settled discipline
Learning Experience Design is best presented as a practical synthesis borrowing from instructional design, UX, service design, learning science, and EdTech. Its defensible distinction is the outcome constraint: the experience must demonstrably support learning.

**Common misconception:** A new professional label automatically means settled methods.

**Worked example:** Use the studio project. Ask what design decision changes if this concept is true. Then create a before/after artifact and a one-sentence Evidence Disclosure. For this chapter, the simplest case is: One product brief rewritten as ID, UX, service design, and LXD.

**Source(s):** See priority sources below.

### Neighboring fields optimize for different questions and outputs
Instructional design asks whether objectives, instruction, practice, and assessment align. UX asks whether an interaction is usable and valuable. Service design maps actors, touchpoints, and backstage systems. LXD asks how all of these shape the learner path toward durable capability.

**Common misconception:** The method is a checklist item rather than a decision tool.

**Worked example:** Use the studio project. Ask what design decision changes if this concept is true. Then create a before/after artifact and a one-sentence Evidence Disclosure. For this chapter, the simplest case is: One product brief rewritten as ID, UX, service design, and LXD.

**Source(s):** See priority sources below.

### Experience is the unit of design, but evidence determines whether the experience teaches
Experience is temporal, social, affective, cognitive, and infrastructural. It includes before-and-after moments that a content outline misses. Evidence keeps experience design from becoming vibe design.

## E. Teaching considerations

**Likely sticking point:** Students will tend to turn this chapter into a checklist. Force them to name the evidence status, the learner data source, and the decision that changes.

**Exercise ideas:**
- Diagnose a flawed artifact from the opening case and label the evidence problem.
- Apply the chapter method to the Track A statistics course.
- Track B: apply the method to the student's own project and write a short Evidence Disclosure.

**LLM guardrail:** Permit AI to generate alternatives or critique clarity, but require the student to provide the evidence label, learner-data source, and final design judgment. Do not let AI replace learner contact, delayed assessment, or ethical review.
