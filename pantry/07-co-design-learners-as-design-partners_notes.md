## A. Conceptual foundations

### Co-design shares problem framing and solution generation
Co-design means learners help frame problems and imagine solutions, not merely react to finished designs. It requires structure and decision-right clarity.

**Common misconception:** Co-design means learners vote and the designer obeys.

**Worked example:** Use the studio project. Ask what design decision changes if this concept is true. Then create a before/after artifact and a one-sentence Evidence Disclosure. For this chapter, the simplest case is: Learners vote to remove retrieval practice; designer preserves it with better support.

**Source(s):** See priority sources below.

### Authority boundaries protect both learner voice and learning evidence
Learners have authority over lived experience, constraints, and meanings. Designers retain responsibility for evidence, ethics, and learning goals.

**Common misconception:** The method is a checklist item rather than a decision tool.

### Ethics are part of method with minors and vulnerable groups
Co-design ethics require consent, role clarity, privacy protection, recognition, and extra care for minors or vulnerable groups.

## E. Teaching considerations

**Likely sticking point:** Students will tend to turn this chapter into a checklist. Force them to name the evidence status, the learner data source, and the decision that changes.

**Exercise ideas:**
- Diagnose a flawed artifact from the opening case and label the evidence problem.
- Apply the chapter method to the Track A statistics course.
- Track B: apply the method to the student's own project and write a short Evidence Disclosure.

**LLM guardrail:** Permit AI to generate alternatives or critique clarity, but require the student to provide the evidence label, learner-data source, and final design judgment. Do not let AI replace learner contact, delayed assessment, or ethical review.
