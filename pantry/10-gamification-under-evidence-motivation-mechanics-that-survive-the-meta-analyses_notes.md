## A. Conceptual foundations

### Positive average effects hide moderator differences
Gamification meta-analyses often show positive averages, but averages hide moderator differences and negative effects. Teach heterogeneity as the headline.

**Common misconception:** Any badge/leaderboard system will work because gamification has positive meta-analytic findings.

**Worked example:** Use the studio project. Ask what design decision changes if this concept is true. Then create a before/after artifact and a one-sentence Evidence Disclosure. For this chapter, the simplest case is: Same badges, different feedback/choice/social design outcomes.

### Points, badges, and leaderboards are not automatically learning mechanics
Points, badges, and leaderboards can provide feedback and goals, but bolted-on rewards can crowd out intrinsic value or demotivate learners who fall behind.

**Common misconception:** Any badge/leaderboard system will work because gamification has positive meta-analytic findings.

**Worked example:** Use the studio project. Ask what design decision changes if this concept is true. Then create a before/after artifact and a one-sentence Evidence Disclosure. For this chapter, the simplest case is: Same badges, different feedback/choice/social design outcomes.

### SDT-aligned gamification supports autonomy, competence, and relatedness
Self-determination theory maps motivation quality to autonomy, competence, and relatedness support. Durable motivation needs meaningful choice, visible progress, and social belonging, not just pressure or reward.

**Common misconception:** Motivation mainly comes from rewards.

**Likely sticking point:** Students will tend to turn this chapter into a checklist. Force them to name the evidence status, the learner data source, and the decision that changes.

**Exercise ideas:**
- Diagnose a flawed artifact from the opening case and label the evidence problem.

**LLM guardrail:** Permit AI to generate alternatives or critique clarity, but require the student to provide the evidence label, learner-data source, and final design judgment. Do not let AI replace learner contact, delayed assessment, or ethical review.
