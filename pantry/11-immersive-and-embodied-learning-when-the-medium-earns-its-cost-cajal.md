## Correction addendum (2026-06-07)

Figure 1 was regenerated on 2026-06-07 after the fact-check pass flagged the opening study as misrepresented. Full-text re-verification of Walters et al. (2026, *Frontiers in Education*, 11:1774496) found that the 31.1% hands-on advantage and the prior-XR-use moderator are genuinely in the paper — but the original figure's two-zone design (with directional, unmeasured prior-XR subgroup dots) and its touch-tank-vs-"VR" framing did not match the study, which compared a headset 360° *video* to a hands-on lesson and reported four measured condition means. The replacement figure (same filename) plots those four published means — pre-test 43.2%, 360° video only 62.1%, hands-on only 83.6%, combined 79.6% — with the authors' own +31.1% figure bracketed, GLMM significance letters, per-condition n, and the combine-media conclusion in the caption. The directional subgroup dots were dropped: no subgroup means are published. The report body above is preserved unedited as the original design rationale; see factchecks/11-immersive-and-embodied-learning-when-the-medium-earns-its-cost-assertions.md (Resolution section) for the full correction trail.
